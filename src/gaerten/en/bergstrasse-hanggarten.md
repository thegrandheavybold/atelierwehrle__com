  copy: |-
    This **hillside garden design on the Bergstrasse** steps down a steep slope below a modern house and ends in a rooftop terrace with far-reaching views over the plain. Rather than levelling the terrain, natural stone walls and broad block steps divide it into small garden rooms linked by gravel paths.

    **The planting design** follows a naturalistic language. Verbena bonariensis, Erigeron karvinskianus and Japanese anemones grow in front of the walls, while thyme, sage and cardoon give the beds their structure. Chard and strawberries show how ornamental and edible planting overlap. A stone trough with a steel spout adds a quiet focal point at the stairs, and on the terrace lavender and grasses stand in large planters.

    The result is a garden in which topography, architecture and **planting design** merge into one naturalistic whole. The Bergstrasse project stands for precise landscape architecture and refined private gardens.
